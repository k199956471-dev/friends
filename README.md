The words leave his mouth and settle near her ear, pulling the air right out of her lungs. "I think we are better off as friends."
Geeta’s blood rushes hot and loud in her ears as she tries to drag oxygen into her chest. Friends?
A flash of memory hits her—that October evening, a light breeze after rain, cold mist rising off the water and swallowing half the town. The old bridge. The cold, rushing water of the Beas below. Standing on the iron railing, fingers wrapped tight around the metal, staring down at the drop. Everything could have ended right there.
"Tum kya kar rahi ho?" a voice calls out from behind her.
She doesn’t turn around. She doesn’t need to, and she doesn’t care. Every old weight presses down at once—screaming parents, beatings, low grades, nights sharp with hunger. None of it matters anymore.
"Dekh, agar tumhe yehi sab karna hai toh tumhein yahan raat ko aana chahiye tha. Abhi toh subah hone waali hai, log baahar nikal rahe hain, tumhein bacha lenge."
Curiosity and annoyance pull at her all the same. "Tumhein koi kaam nahin hai subah-subah? Jo random logon ke peeche pade ho?"
Out of the corner of her eye, she catches him leaning against the side of the bridge with his arms crossed.
"Arre, kya hua? Koi chhod ke chala gaya, chhalaang lagane chali ho?"
Pain hides underneath the anger and irritation sparked by that smirk on his face.
"Ya exam mein low rank aayi hai? Tum lagti toh college student hi ho."
She drops down from the railing, letting the edge go for a moment, and lunges at him with a jab aimed straight at his jaw to wipe that smirk off. He catches sight of her moving and stumbles backward just to stay out of the way.
"Arre, arre—"
She doesn’t land a clean punch, though her nails catch the skin along his jaw. He groans. "Ouch! Bandariya!"
He rants, rubbing his jaw. "Tumhari dikkat kya hai? Ek toh main—" He stops, looking at her blood-streaked fingertips before touching his cheek again. "Ouch, ouch! Tera dimaag kharab hai kya?"
Her chest rises and falls fast, and she doesn’t stop. She lunges forward again with both hands out. He scrambles to dodge, and the two of them stumble toward one side of the bridge, her hair tangled and loose around her face. He tries to keep her from tearing at his face, catching her wrists without wanting to really hurt her.
Below them, the river keeps tearing past. All she has is the grinding of her teeth and eyes wide with raw, cornered fury.
"Paagal ho gayi hai kya? Maine sirf thoda hi toh bola tha, ismein itna gussa kaun karta hai?"
She fights like she’s ready to break or be broken, putting every ounce of strength into raking her nails across his hands. He hisses sharply as the skin gives way. Tears spill down her face while she pushes her weight down harder to make it hurt, her arms starting to shake even as rage forces more pressure behind them.
Then she catches the shift in his face—fear giving way to sadness.
Her knees give out. She drops to the stone, crying hard, her nails catching against the skin of her own neck until beads of blood well up as she yells in anger.
For the first few seconds, the shock hits him like whiplash, as if someone just dragged him straight into freezing water. He holds his ground.
"Sunno, sab sahi hai."
